Fort Lauderdale is a city of water: the New River through downtown, finger-island canals off Las Olas, the Intracoastal and the Atlantic. Which neighborhood fits you usually comes down to how close you want to be to the boulevard, whether a boat is in the picture, and whether you want a condo, a townhome or a house with a yard and a seawall. Here's my read on six areas buyers ask me about most, with the numbers to back it up.

A caution on two rows: Las Olas Isles and Rio Vista are small samples, with 11 and 14 sales in August 2026. Medians that thin can swing month to month, so treat them as a sense of scale rather than a price tag. Every median describes what sold, not what a particular home is worth.

## Las Olas and Downtown: the boulevard, the river and the Isles

Las Olas Boulevard's shops and restaurants, the Riverwalk along the New River, and Brightline's Fort Lauderdale station a short walk from the downtown towers. This is the most urban way to live in the city.

**Housing.** Downtown condos and new towers along the river. The Las Olas Isles are single-family waterfront homes on finger islands with deep-water canal frontage and ocean access, which explains that $5.2M median.

**Getting around.** About 15 minutes to Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International (FLL) and about 40 minutes to Brickell, off-peak[^9]. Brightline is steps away.

**Know before you buy.** The condo stock mixes new towers with older mid-rises, so compare reserves and insurance costs building by building. If you're thinking about short stays, Fort Lauderdale requires vacation rentals to register with the city, and many associations add their own limits.

## Victoria Park: historic streets, minutes from everything

Tree-lined streets between downtown and the beach, close to Holiday Park and Las Olas, where older cottages sit beside new contemporary builds.

**Housing.** Single-family homes and modern townhomes, with small condo buildings scattered throughout. It's mostly inland, with some canal-front homes at the edges.

**Getting around.** About 5 minutes to downtown and about 15 to FLL[^9].

**Know before you buy.** Small, older condo buildings can mean large per-unit assessments, so get the reports. If you're buying with redevelopment in mind, check the zoning first.

## Rio Vista and Tarpon River: river lots and old-Florida character

Rio Vista's shaded streets and riverfront estates sit just south of Las Olas. Tarpon River, along the river of the same name, is quieter and more mixed.

**Housing.** Mostly single-family. Rio Vista has large waterfront homes, many with deep-water dockage on the New River, the Tarpon River or the Intracoastal. Tarpon River has smaller houses, duplexes and small condo buildings, which is why its median sits at $595K against Rio Vista's $2.2M.

**Getting around.** About 5 minutes to downtown and about 15 to FLL from Rio Vista[^9].

**Know before you buy.** Condos are limited here. The bigger due-diligence items are seawalls, docks and flood zones. Rio Vista is a market of people buying to live there. Tarpon River offers lower entry points one bridge from Las Olas.

## Fort Lauderdale Beach and the Galt Ocean Mile

The A1A promenade near Las Olas Beach, then north to the quieter Galt Ocean Mile, a long row of oceanfront condos with its own neighborhood shops.

**Housing.** Mostly condos, from older oceanfront high-rises on the Galt to new full-service towers near the beach center, plus some homes and townhomes on the Intracoastal side.

**Getting around.** About 15 minutes to downtown and about 25 to FLL from the beach[^9].

**Know before you buy.** Much of the Galt's oceanfront stock dates from the 1960s to the 1980s. Florida requires milestone inspections for condo buildings of three or more habitable stories at 30 years of age and every 10 years after, plus a structural integrity reserve study at least every 10 years[^10]. On the Galt, those reports and any reserve-driven assessments are central to pricing. Seasonal rental demand is real, but city registration and building rules decide what's possible.

## Wilton Manors: the Island City

Wrapped by the Middle River, with Wilton Drive's restaurants, bars and events as the social spine and a compact, walkable small-city layout.

**Housing.** Mostly single-family: mid-century ranches, renovated cottages and riverfront homes, plus townhomes and small condos. Many streets have Middle River frontage. Boaters should check bridge clearances before they fall for a dock.

**Getting around.** About 10 minutes to downtown Fort Lauderdale and about 20 to FLL[^9].

**Know before you buy.** Small, low-rise associations are common here, so ask for the budget, reserve balance and insurance details. Check the city's rental rules if income is part of the plan.

## How to choose

**If you want to walk to dinner and take the train,** start with Las Olas and Downtown.

**If you want a house near downtown without a condo board,** look at Victoria Park and Tarpon River.

**If the boat matters as much as the house,** Rio Vista and the Las Olas Isles are where deep water meets downtown. Budget for seawall and dock inspections.

**If you want to wake up to the ocean,** compare the new towers near the beach center with the established buildings on the Galt, and read every building's reports.

**If you want a main street with its own pace,** Wilton Manors puts one at your door, about 10 minutes from downtown.

Whatever the area, I run the same checks before an offer: flood zone and insurance quotes for the exact address, seawall and dock condition on waterfront lots, association documents for condos and HOAs, and city and building rental rules if income matters. In Fort Lauderdale, the water adds value to almost every property, and it adds homework too.
