If you're shopping for a condo in Miami, the short list usually comes down to two names: Brickell and Edgewater. Both sit on Biscayne Bay. Both are almost entirely condos. Right now they trade at nearly the same price per square foot. The real differences are how you'll live day to day, what kind of building you're buying into, and what that building's paperwork says. Here's how I walk clients through the choice.

## The numbers, side by side

These are medians for all home types, pulled from Redfin's neighborhood pages. Redfin draws the boundaries, and I've added Downtown Miami because it sits between the two and often ends up in the same search.

| Metric | Brickell | Edgewater | Downtown Miami |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median sale price | $660K | $548K | $580K |
| Median price per sq ft | $585 | $588 | $589 |
| Period | Trailing 3 mo., Sept 2026 update | Trailing 3 mo., Sept 2026 update | 3 mo. through Aug 2026 |

Sources: Redfin[^1][^2][^3].

Two things stand out. First, price per square foot is within $4 across all three. On a per-foot basis, neither neighborhood is the bargain. Second, the $112K gap between the Brickell and Edgewater medians says more about the size and mix of the units that sold than about one area being cheaper. A median describes what sold in a given window. It doesn't tell you what a specific unit is worth, so we price every building on its own comparable sales.

## Brickell: Miami's vertical downtown

Brickell is built for walking. Glass towers, sidewalk cafés and a financial district that turns into rooftop bars at dusk. Brickell City Centre, Mary Brickell Village and the free Metromover keep daily errands on foot, and the Metrorail's Brickell station connects you to the rest of the county.

**What you're buying.** Almost entirely condos, from 1980s mid-rises to new full-service and branded towers, plus a few single-family streets toward The Roads. The bayfront buildings on Brickell Bay Drive and Brickell Key face Biscayne Bay. You pay for that view. Private dockage is rare, so if a boat is part of the plan, Brickell usually isn't where it lives.

**Getting around.** In off-peak traffic, the center of Brickell is about 25 minutes from Miami International Airport, 35 minutes from Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International and 40 minutes from downtown Fort Lauderdale. Brightline's MiamiCentral station is about 5 minutes away[^6].

**If you're investing.** Renter demand follows the office core. Many Brickell buildings set minimum lease terms or restrict short stays, so read each building's rules before you model any income.

## Edgewater: the bayfront corridor, still being built

Edgewater sits just north of downtown and trades office energy for bayfront towers, Margaret Pace Park and a quick hop to the Design District. Directly south, downtown brings Bayfront Park, the Kaseya Center, PAMM and Frost Science within reach.

**What you're buying.** Mostly condos. New and pre-construction towers line the bay, with older mid-rises and low-rise pockets west of Biscayne Boulevard. Edgewater has direct Biscayne Bay frontage, and here too the view is the waterfront asset. Boat slips are limited.

**Getting around.** From the Downtown and Edgewater area, Brickell is about 5 minutes away, MIA about 25 and FLL about 35, all off-peak. Metromover, Metrorail and Brightline at MiamiCentral are all nearby[^6].

**If you're investing.** The tower pipeline means resale and rental units compete with each other. Buy for the view, the floor plan and the quality of the building, not just the address.

## The building matters more than the neighborhood

This is the part most comparisons skip. After the 2021 Champlain Towers South collapse in Surfside, Florida changed how condo buildings are inspected and how repairs are funded. Those rules now show up in prices, dues and financing.

- **Milestone inspections.** Condo and co-op buildings of three or more habitable stories need one when they reach 30 years of age, then every 10 years. A local building department can require the first one at 25 years[^4].
- **Structural integrity reserve studies (SIRS).** Condo associations with buildings of three or more habitable stories must complete a SIRS at least every 10 years. Associations that existed on or before July 1, 2022 and are controlled by unit owners had to have one done by December 31, 2025[^4].
- **What a SIRS covers.** The roof, structure, fireproofing and fire protection, plumbing, electrical, waterproofing and exterior painting, windows and exterior doors, and other structural items above a cost threshold[^5].

**How that plays out here.** Brickell has a wide age spread. Older bayfront buildings fall squarely under these rules, while new towers carry full-service dues. In Edgewater, the wave of brand-new inventory puts milestone deadlines years out. Even so, read the developer's budget and reserve plan, and expect dues to change once owners take control from the developer.

Before you write an offer in either neighborhood, ask for:

1. The milestone inspection summary (Phase 1, and Phase 2 if it was required)
2. The latest SIRS and its funding plan
3. The current budget and reserve balances
4. Any approved or proposed special assessments, loans or lines of credit
5. The master insurance policy, deductibles and recent premium changes
6. Twelve months of board minutes
7. Lease, rental and pet rules
8. Any pending litigation

Lenders and insurers read the same reports. A weak building can be harder to finance, and that affects you on the way in and on the way out.

## Which one fits how you live

**Brickell fits if** you want everything on foot, the office core around the corner, Metrorail at your door and a deep, established resale market across every price point and building age.

**Edgewater fits if** you want newer construction on the bay, a park on the water, the arts district to the south and the Design District minutes away, and you're comfortable with a neighborhood that's still filling in.

**Downtown deserves a look if** Brightline is part of your week. MiamiCentral puts Fort Lauderdale, Boca Raton and West Palm Beach on the train.

## How I'd shop it

Start with a total monthly number, not a list price: the mortgage, dues, insurance and taxes together. Then shortlist buildings rather than neighborhoods, because two towers on the same block can carry very different reserves, assessments and rental rules. Pull the association documents before you fall for a view, and check the flood zone for the exact address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center[^7]. If you plan to rent the unit, confirm the lease minimums in writing first.

Brickell and Edgewater are both strong choices. The right one is the one whose buildings hold up to that checklist and whose streets match the way you actually spend your days.
