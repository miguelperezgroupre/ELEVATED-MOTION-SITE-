**Short answer:** people who know Miami from vacations tend to get three things wrong. First, "Miami" isn't one market. Neighborhoods differ sharply in price, pace and housing type. Second, the list price isn't the real cost. Insurance, condo dues and a property tax bill that resets at purchase shape what you actually pay each month. Third, weather is a planning question, not a dealbreaker. Hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, and flood risk is set by each address, not by the city as a whole. Here's what that looks like day to day.

## 1. "Miami" is many markets, not one

Brickell and Aventura are about half an hour apart, yet they trade at very different prices. Brickell's median was about **$615 per square foot** on Redfin's October 2026 page, while Aventura's was about **$333**[^1][^2]. The way you live differs just as much:

- **Brickell and Downtown:** vertical, walkable and transit-connected (Metromover, Metrorail and Brightline nearby). Almost all condos.
- **Edgewater:** bayfront towers, many new or pre-construction, near the Design District and Wynwood.
- **Coral Gables and Coconut Grove:** tree canopy, single-family streets, older homes and a slower pace.
- **Aventura and the northeast corridor:** larger condo layouts, Intracoastal views and more space per dollar.

What to do: choose a neighborhood by how you'll spend a typical Tuesday, including your commute, errands, schools and noise, not by the skyline. Start with my [neighborhood guides](/neighborhoods), and for two areas head-to-head, read [Brickell vs. Edgewater](/journal/brickell-vs-edgewater-miami-condos).

## 2. The list price isn't the real cost

Newcomers compare list prices. Locals compare monthly costs. Three line items are easy to underestimate.

- **Property taxes reset when you buy.** Florida's Save Our Homes rule limits annual increases in a homesteaded home's assessed value, but the assessment resets to market value after a sale[^3]. The seller's tax bill is not your tax bill.
- **Insurance.** Homeowners and condo policies, including wind coverage, are a major cost in South Florida. Get quotes before you're under contract. Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation has consumer guides on coverage and shopping for a policy[^5].
- **Condo dues and reserves.** Since the post-Surfside reforms, many condo associations must complete structural integrity reserve studies and fund reserves for the roof, structure, waterproofing and other key items. Milestone inspections are required at 30 years, then every 10[^4]. In some buildings that has meant higher dues or special assessments.

What to do: build a full monthly budget (mortgage, taxes at the new assessed value, insurance, HOA and flood coverage if needed) before you pick a price range. If you already own here, a [home value estimate](/home-value) is a quick starting point.

## 3. Weather is a planning question

**Hurricane season** in the Atlantic runs June 1 to November 30, with most activity in late summer and early fall[^6]. Residents plan around it. They know their evacuation zone, keep supplies on hand and pay attention to how a building is built: impact windows or shutters, roof age and generator backup in condos.

**Flood risk depends on the address.** Two homes a few blocks apart can sit in different FEMA flood zones. Check the exact property on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center[^7], ask the seller about flood history and get a flood insurance quote early. Lenders may require flood coverage in high-risk zones.

**Seasons are real, just different.** Winter brings visitors, events and traffic. Summer is hotter and wetter and a little quieter. Many locals plan travel, renovations and moves with that rhythm in mind.

## Bottom line

Miami rewards people who look past the postcard. Pick a neighborhood for how you'll live, budget for the full monthly cost and check each building and address for its specific risks. Do that, and the lifestyle that drew you here holds up long after the move.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Miami one housing market?
No. Neighborhoods vary widely. In October 2026, Brickell's median was about $615 per square foot versus about $333 in Aventura, and lifestyles range from walkable high-rises to tree-lined single-family streets.

### Why do property taxes go up after buying a home in Florida?
Florida's Save Our Homes limit caps yearly increases in a homesteaded home's assessed value, but the assessment resets to market value after a sale. A new owner's bill can therefore be much higher than the seller's.

### When is hurricane season in Miami?
The Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30, according to NOAA's National Hurricane Center. Activity usually peaks in late summer and early fall.

### How do I check whether a Miami home is in a flood zone?
Search the exact address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center, ask the seller about flood history and get a flood insurance quote before you commit.
