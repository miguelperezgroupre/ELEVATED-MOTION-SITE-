**Short answer:** waiting for lower rates can cost you more than it saves, but not always. Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed rate at **7.24% on October 29, 2026**, down from 7.32% the week before and up from 6.17% a year earlier[^1]. If you wait, three things work against you. Prices can keep rising, more buyers return when rates fall, and you lose the negotiating room today's slower market offers. If you buy at a payment you can afford, you can refinance later if rates drop, but you can't get back a price increase. Nobody can promise where rates or prices will go, so decide on your own payment and timeline, not a forecast.

Rates also move fast. PBP Real Estate notes that rates fell below 6% in late February 2026 and were back near their 2026 high by fall[^2]. That's a good illustration of how hard they are to time.

## Argument 1: prices can rise while you wait

NAR's quarterly report showed Miami single-family prices up 3.3% over the past year, nearly twice the national pace, with 18 of 19 Florida markets posting gains[^5]. Here's a hypothetical to make it concrete (not a forecast):

| Scenario ($750K home, 20% down) | Loan | Monthly P&I |
|---|---|---|
| Buy now at 7.24% | $600,000 | ≈ $4,089 |
| Wait: rate falls to 6.24%, price unchanged | $600,000 | ≈ $3,690 |
| Wait: rate falls to 6.24%, price up 3% | $618,000 | ≈ $3,801 |

*Principal and interest only. Taxes, insurance and HOA dues are excluded. The figures are illustrative arithmetic, not a quote.*

In this example, a full one-point drop still saves money, but a 3% price rise eats part of it and adds $4,500 to the down payment. If rates don't fall, or prices rise faster, waiting costs more.

## Argument 2: lower rates bring back competition

Right now buyers have leverage. NAR reported more inventory, longer days on market and the highest share of price cuts for a September since 2018[^4]. PBP makes the point that lower rates usually bring more buyers back into the same inventory. That can offset the rate savings through price and lost negotiating leverage[^3]. Negotiating room on price, repairs and seller concessions tends to be greatest when other buyers are sitting out.

## Argument 3: you can refinance later, with care

If you buy now and rates fall meaningfully, refinancing can lower your payment. PBP sums it up: if rates fall, refinancing is available; if prices rise while you wait, that gain isn't recoverable[^3]. Two caveats:

- Refinancing has closing costs and depends on qualifying at the time, including your credit, income and home value.
- Be wary of high-pressure refinance offers. The Miami Herald reported on aggressive refi sales tactics and urged borrowers to compare options and take their time[^6].

## When waiting does make sense

This isn't a case for buying no matter what. Waiting is reasonable if:

- The payment would strain your budget at today's rate.
- You might move again within a few years, since closing costs eat into short holds.
- Your savings would be wiped out by the down payment.
- You haven't found the right home. Buying the wrong property to beat a rate is worse.

In South Florida, insurance, HOA dues and taxes often swing the monthly cost as much as a quarter-point of rate, so price those early[^2].

## A practical plan

1. Get pre-approved and stress-test your payment at a higher rate.
2. Add up the full monthly cost: principal and interest, taxes, insurance and HOA.
3. Shop for the right property, then negotiate while you have leverage.
4. Keep an eye on rates after closing and refinance only if the math clearly works.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the current 30-year mortgage rate?
Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey reported an average 30-year fixed rate of 7.24% for the week of October 29, 2026, and 6.60% for the 15-year fixed.

### Is it better to wait for mortgage rates to drop before buying?
Not necessarily. Waiting can mean higher prices and more competition when rates fall. Buying now and refinancing later can work if the payment is affordable today. It depends on your budget, timeline and the home, and no one can predict rates.

### Can I refinance if rates go down after I buy?
Usually, if you qualify at that time and the savings outweigh the closing costs. Compare several lenders and avoid high-pressure offers.

### Are Miami home prices still rising?
NAR's latest quarterly report showed Miami single-family existing-home prices up 3.3% year over year. Condo segments vary by neighborhood and building.
