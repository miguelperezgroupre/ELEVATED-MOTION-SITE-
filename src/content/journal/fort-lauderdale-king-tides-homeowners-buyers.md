Fall in South Florida brings king tides, the highest tides of the year. The City of Fort Lauderdale expects higher tides **Oct. 24–31 and Nov. 5–11, 2026**, with **some street flooding**, according to the Fort Lauderdale Journal.

## During the king tides

- **Report impassable roads** to the city at **954-828-8000**.
- **Don't drive through standing water.** Saltwater can damage vehicles, and you can't see how deep the water is.
- **Plan your timing.** Flooding from high tides tends to come and go with the tide cycle, so a route that's clear in the morning may be wet later in the day.
- **Boat show week overlaps.** The Fort Lauderdale International Boat Show (Oct. 28–Nov. 1) also falls in the first window, and heavy traffic is expected near the beach and the 17th Street Causeway.

## What homeowners and buyers should know

King tides show you how a neighborhood handles high water. If you own a home or are shopping for one, they're a useful time to look. This is general guidance only. For anything specific to a property, talk to the right licensed professionals.

**1. Know the flood zone.**
Look up a property's flood zone designation on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov). The zone can affect insurance requirements and costs, so check it early in your search.

**2. Ask about elevation.**
Ask whether an **elevation certificate** is available. It documents a building's elevation and can be helpful in insurance conversations.

**3. Look at the seawall.**
For waterfront homes, the seawall's condition and height matter. Ask about its age, any repairs, and whether local requirements apply. A qualified marine contractor or inspector can assess it.

**4. Ask insurance questions before you commit.**
Get flood insurance quotes during your due diligence period, not after closing. Questions to ask:
- Is flood insurance required for this property?
- Is there a flood claims history?
- How do the flood zone and elevation affect the premium?

**5. See the street in person.**
Driving the neighborhood during a king tide shows you how the streets and driveways around a home handle high water. You won't get that from a listing.

**6. Read the disclosures.**
Review the seller's disclosures and ask direct questions about any past water intrusion.
